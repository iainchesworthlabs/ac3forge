Which copy of Qt runs depends on which download this is, and the two
Linux downloads differ:

  The .deb, .rpm and tarball include no Qt at all. ac3gui loads the Qt 6
  libraries and QML modules your distribution provides - the .deb and
  .rpm name them as package dependencies - so the version that runs is
  the one installed on your machine, and your distribution's own Qt
  packages carry the licence texts and the source.

  The AppImage carries its own copy of the Qt libraries, plugins and QML
  modules ac3gui loads, put there at build time by linuxdeploy's Qt
  plugin, so that it runs on a machine where Qt was never installed.
  That copy is what the paragraphs below are about.

The Qt libraries are linked dynamically in both. You may replace the
AppImage's copy with a modified Qt 6 that is binary-compatible with this
version: unpack the image (--appimage-extract), replace the libraries in
usr/lib and the QML modules in usr/qml, and run the unpacked AppRun.

The complete corresponding source of this exact Qt version is published
by The Qt Company at
https://download.qt.io/archive/qt/{{QT_SERIES}}/{{QT_VERSION}}/single/
and the project will also supply it on request, through the repository's
issue tracker, for as long as this package is offered.

The Qt libraries in turn contain third-party code, each part under its
own terms - PCRE2, zlib, FreeType, HarfBuzz, libpng, libjpeg-turbo, the
Unicode character database and some dozens more. The SPDX documents in
the Qt {{QT_VERSION}} kit this package was built against (sbom/*.spdx)
are the record of which of those it carries and under what terms, and
Qt's attribution for each, with the full text of every licence named, is
at https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/licenses-used-in-qt.html
